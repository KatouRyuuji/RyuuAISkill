Before rewriting the prompt, clarify the task and raise its ambition:

1. Restate in plain language what I want, what problem I'm trying to solve, and what I may actually mean. Trace the requested task to the result it should produce and the purpose that result serves. Ask when the intent is unclear. Do not assume the requested artifact is the best way to serve that purpose.
2. Aim for the strongest worthwhile outcome under my constraints, not merely a better-worded version of the requested task. When changing the task itself would create more value, reframe "complete X" as "achieve Y and verify Z". Raising the aim is not permission to expand scope: if the higher-value framing changes the deliverable, resources, or constraints, explain the tradeoff and offer faithful and elevated options instead of choosing for me.
3. Ground the rewrite in top professional practice. Before rewriting, say:
   - What would a top professional do that I am not asking for?
   - What raw materials or primary evidence would they start from?
   - What specific methods and professional tools would they use?
   - Which assumptions or accepted conclusions should be tested rather than trusted?
   - What external evidence would show the result is right? If no external check is available, call for an independent second review and state its limits.
4. When it would materially improve the result, borrow specific methods from adjacent fields and explain how to apply them. Do not force extra perspectives or substitute role-playing for real methods.
5. Rewrite my prompt around that understanding. Lead with the final outcome, then the execution path. Include the context, output specification, and constraints needed to act. Set concrete, verifiable success criteria at the level of an excellent result, not mere completion; quantify when meaningful. Mark missing information with `[Please provide: ...]` instead of inventing it. Leave choices that are mine to make open.

Make the prompt higher in aim and deeper in method, not merely bigger in deliverables. Match the treatment to the stakes: keep simple tasks light, and give complex or important tasks deeper reframing, professional methods, and stronger validation. Every sentence must change behavior.

Before returning, check the rewritten prompt against the purpose, professional method, external evidence, and my original intent. Revise any gaps. Do not execute the rewritten task.
